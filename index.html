My introduction

I learned to see the whole problem.

A good solution begins with understanding the whole problem: how a process works, why it needs to change, and who the change affects.

My B.S. in Engineering taught me to examine processes and improve them. My M.A. in International Development and Project Management expanded my view to complex programs, giving me tools to identify root causes, understand cause and effect, and turn goals into project plans.

My M.S. in Technology and Innovation Enterprise brought the focus to the people who use a solution through product design, design thinking, and entrepreneurship. My M.S. in Data Science and Analytics taught me to investigate evidence through statistical analysis and exploratory data analysis, then build machine learning models with R and Python. I am now pursuing my M.S. in Artificial Intelligence, extending that foundation through Java, object oriented programming, and machine learning.

Together, these fields shape how I work: I look at the system, consider the people it affects, follow the evidence, and build toward a practical solution.

Experience in action

Program Management is part of my data story.

I connect analysis with a clear plan and learning designed for its audience.

I brought these perspectives together in a global leadership program. I analyzed five years of program data using SQL and presented the findings to leadership. My analysis and presentation made the case for improvement and prompted leadership to strengthen the program. Following root cause analysis, I developed an enhancement project plan with eight outcomes and 75 activities. One outcome was a workbook for the training materials. I applied design thinking through empathy, product design, prototyping, testing, and iterative improvement. Audience analysis showed that the lengthy, text-heavy format was not a good fit for the intended learners. I then used retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) to streamline the content.

DSO Daddy: An AI Student Guide Prototype

An independent chatbot prototype created to help international students find starting points for common university and student-support questions.

Explore the chatbot prototype
View artifact details

Introduction: DSO Daddy explores how a conversational AI prototype could make common student information easier to navigate.

Description: The prototype focuses on student questions such as immigration processes, enrollment, employment, housing, and campus support.

Objective: Create an accessible starting point for students seeking answers to recurring questions.

Process: Identified student information needs, researched the topic with ChatGPT, and deployed the prototype with Lovable.

Tools and technologies: ChatGPT for research; Lovable for deployment.

Unique value: Applies conversational AI to a real information-access challenge affecting international students.

Relevance: Demonstrates problem framing, applied AI exploration, and attention to user needs and responsible use.

AI disclaimer: This independent coursework prototype may provide inaccurate or outdated information. It is not an official IWU service and does not provide legal advice.
