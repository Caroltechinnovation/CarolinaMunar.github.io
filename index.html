Experience in action

Program Management is part of my data story.

Insight became a case for change, then a plan designed around its learners.

I brought these perspectives together in a global leadership program. I analyzed five years of program data using SQL and presented the findings to leadership. My analysis and presentation made the case for improvement and prompted leadership to strengthen the program. Following root cause analysis, I developed an enhancement project plan with eight outcomes and 75 activities. One outcome was a workbook for the training materials. I applied design thinking through empathy, product design, prototyping, testing, and iterative improvement. Audience analysis showed that the lengthy, text-heavy format was not a good fit for the intended learners. I then used retrieval augmented generation (RAG) to streamline the content.

Artifact one · Artificial Intelligence

From Logic to Language: A Visual History of AI

A visual journey through the ideas, methods, and turning points that shaped artificial intelligence.

Artificial Intelligence: 1943 to today

From mathematical models of neurons to systems that generate language, images, and multimodal responses.

01 · Foundations1943–1955 · Logic, computation, neuron models
02 · Symbolic AI1956–1960s · Rules, search, language programs
03 · AI winters and expert systems1970s–1980s · Cycles of promise and limits
04 · Statistical machine learning1980s–2000s · Learning patterns from data
05 · Deep learning2010s · Neural networks at scale
06 · Generative AI2020s–today · Foundation and multimodal models
  1. 1943

    A mathematical model of a neuron

    Warren McCulloch and Walter Pitts describe how networks of simplified neurons could perform logical operations, connecting computation and ideas about nervous activity.

  2. 1945

    The stored-program computer takes shape

    John von Neumann circulates the First Draft of a Report on the EDVAC, describing a design in which instructions and data can be stored in computer memory.

  3. 1950

    Turing asks whether machines can think

    Alan Turing reframes the question through an imitation game, giving later researchers a concrete way to discuss machine intelligence.

  4. 1956

    Dartmouth gives the field a name

    The Dartmouth summer research project establishes “artificial intelligence” as a research agenda and brings together early leaders in the field.

  5. 1958

    Perceptron and LISP

    Frank Rosenblatt’s perceptron advances trainable pattern recognition, while John McCarthy’s LISP supports symbolic computation and AI research.

  6. 1966

    ELIZA makes conversation a visible AI idea

    Joseph Weizenbaum’s program uses written patterns to simulate dialogue, prompting questions about how people interpret machine responses.

  7. 1970s–80s

    Expert systems rise as AI winters expose limits

    Rule-based systems show practical promise in specialized domains. Funding and expectations contract when results do not match broad claims.

  8. 1986

    Backpropagation strengthens neural network learning

    Research by David Rumelhart, Geoffrey Hinton, and Ronald Williams helps make multilayer neural networks more practical to train.

  9. 1997

    Deep Blue defeats Garry Kasparov

    IBM’s chess system demonstrates the power of specialized search and computation in a highly visible human–machine contest.

  10. 2012

    AlexNet accelerates deep learning

    A deep convolutional neural network achieves a major ImageNet result, highlighting the combined impact of data, GPUs, and neural network methods.

  11. 2017

    The Transformer architecture

    “Attention Is All You Need” introduces a model architecture that becomes a foundation for many later large language models.

  12. 2022–today

    Generative and multimodal AI enters everyday use

    Conversational systems and foundation models bring text, image, audio, and code generation to broader audiences, while governance and responsible deployment become central questions.

The timeline highlights selected milestones; it is a learning artifact, not an exhaustive history. The distinct era colors are used only inside this infographic to help readers follow the historical shifts.

Artifact information

Introduction: This infographic helps a broad audience see how ideas and technologies in AI developed over time.

Description: A six-era visual timeline connecting foundational theories, symbolic programs, machine learning, deep learning, and generative AI.

Objective: Explain key AI milestones and advances in a clear, insightful, and visually engaging way.

Process: Researched the history with BoodleBox AI, selected and organized milestones, designed the infographic in Venngage, and adapted its key eras into this web timeline. The visual groups developments by shifts in how AI systems represent, learn, and generate information.

Tools and technologies: BoodleBox AI for research; Venngage for infographic design; HTML and CSS for the on-page timeline.

Unique value: Turns a broad technical history into an accessible visual narrative of changing approaches to intelligence.

Relevance: Demonstrates research synthesis, historical perspective, and communication of complex AI ideas.

Reflection: Organizing the milestones by conceptual shifts helped me see how the field moved from hand-defined rules toward systems that learn patterns and generate new content. It also showed me how a visual structure can make technical change easier to follow.

Artifact two · Applied AI

DSO Daddy: An AI Student Guide Prototype

A class project exploring how conversational AI might help international students navigate common questions.

Introduction: DSO Daddy is an independent chatbot prototype designed around common information needs of international students.

Explore the chatbot prototype
Artifact information

Description: A conversational prototype that points students toward information about F-1 status, CPT, OPT, travel, admissions, employment, housing, and campus support.

Objective: Explore a responsive starting point for repeated student questions while making clear when a student needs individual support from an official source.

Process: Used empathy to define the information challenge, identified common question areas, researched content with ChatGPT, shaped the chatbot experience, deployed it with Lovable, and explored sample questions during testing.

Tools and technologies: ChatGPT for research; Lovable for deployment.

Unique value: Applies conversational AI to a real student information challenge and considers both access and appropriate boundaries.

Relevance: Demonstrates user-centered problem framing, applied AI exploration, prototyping, and attention to responsible communication.

AI disclaimer: This independent coursework prototype may provide inaccurate or outdated information. It is not an official IWU service and does not provide legal advice. Confirm university questions with the appropriate IWU office and immigration questions with the DSO or an official government source.

Reflection: This project reinforced that a useful chatbot needs more than fluent responses. It needs clear boundaries, reliable sources, and a path to human support for individual immigration questions.

References: Indiana Wesleyan University, DHS Study in the States, and U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services.
